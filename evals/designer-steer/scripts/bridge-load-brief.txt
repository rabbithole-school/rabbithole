BUILD BRIEF: Bridge Load App

Context: The scholar has spent two sessions studying how bridges and trusses carry weight. They sketched a small truss and wrote down which beams they think work hardest and why. Now they direct the AI builder to turn THEIR bridge into a small interactive app. The scholar is the designer and director; the builder writes the code. The scholar owns every design decision.

(a) What the app must let the scholar demonstrate
- Their bridge: a few beams (members) joined at points, with a place for weight to sit.
- How a load placed on the bridge is shared among the members it connects to.
- What happens when a member is pushed past its strength: it breaks, and the load it carried moves onto its neighbours.
- A way to TEST it: add weight, move the weight, make one beam stronger or weaker, and see which member fails first.

(b) Learning arc (discover by building and observing, in this order)
1. Start from their own sketch. Ask the scholar which beams they think carry the most, in their own words, before anything is built.
2. Build the plain beam first, with nothing special: one beam, a weight, load it until it breaks. Let them watch what happens.
3. Add a second member. Have them PREDICT where the load goes before they run it, then compare.
4. Break one member on purpose and ask what happened to its neighbours.
5. Only then: look, story, vehicles, extras.

(c) What the builder must not do
- Do not explain the rule before the scholar has seen it.
- Do not let cosmetics come before the plain beam has been loaded once.
