# 1. Validity

**Student utterance relates to mathematics** (1 = yes, 0 = no). Mark yes if the student's utterance pertains to the mathematical content of the lesson, including asking for clarification or showing lack of understanding ("I don't understand.", "Could you repeat the question?", "I can't do that." about a math procedure). Not related: "Can I go to the bathroom?", "Should I bring my scissors?"

**Teacher utterance relates to mathematics** (1 = yes, 0 = no). Mark yes if the teacher's utterance pertains to the mathematical content of the lesson. Not related: "Why didn't you do your homework?", "Please raise your hand.", "Sit down.", "Can you help me turn on the microphone?" Related: "Please check your answers.", "Discuss your solution with your neighbor.", "Write down your answer.", "Let me know if anything is unclear." If an utterance relates to mathematics even partially, mark it as related.

Code active listening for every exchange, even when a validity item is 0.

# 2. Display of active listening (uptake)

To what degree does the teacher show that they are listening to the student's idea?

## High (2)
The teacher provides substantial evidence that they are following what the student is saying or trying to say, by engaging with the student's contribution.

- Synthesis and/or complex reformulation.
  - S: "Uh the first 2 numbers the 3 and the 6 in each problem." T: "Okay so Student K is noticing we have a 6 and we have a 3, What's the relationship between the 6 and the 3?"
  - S: "It's like both the length and like both lengths, like top and bottom, and you add those together and add that to the width and like both sides of width. well, add both sides of the width together and add both of totals together." T: "Okay, that would give you the perimeter of something like a - it sounds a lot like you're describing a what?"
- Guiding the student's thoughts.
  - S: "So if you have 20, it will be 2,000." T: "So if I doubled this end."
  - S: "- 30 plus 20 and then -" T: "which is"
- Answering the student's question via engagement/demonstration.
  - S: "Um, actually like for the square isn't it, isn't it three lines of symmetry?" T: "1, 2, 3, 4."
- Repetition, showing consideration of the student's idea.
  - S: "You could draw a triangle, too." T: "You could also do a triangle, yep."
  - S: "They'll get mixed up." T: "Why would it get mixed up?"

## Mid (1)
The teacher provides some evidence that they are following what the student is saying or trying to say, but the response is more cursory and/or has less engagement with what the student said.

- Surface-level reformulation.
  - S: "So you have to subtract." T: "Someone say 100 minus 30 cents?"
- Teacher says or asks something related to the student's utterance, but without really acknowledging the student's contribution.
  - S: "Added the three holes and then I used the fours and added it to the one eighth." T: "Who can explain that to me a little more clearly?"
- Simple evaluation (acceptance / rejection).
  - S: "And this and the bottom of flat." T: "Excellent."
  - S: "Okay, I did it like this." T: "No, no, no. This is a centimeter."
  - S: "And then we did seven times six is 42." T: "Okay"
- Repetition, showing more surface-level consideration of the student's idea.
  - S: "Oh yeah, you add them." T: "Okay. When you're adding decimals, you line them up. What about when you're subtracting? Student E?"
  - S: "24 divided by 9." T: "No. Divided by –"
  - S: "And losses. Games.." T: "No. Total number of games. Total number of games. And my number of wins -"

## Low (0)
The teacher does not provide evidence, or provides only very little evidence, that they are following what the student is saying or trying to say.

- The teacher does not make it clear that they heard the student.
  - S: "Two quarts." T: "Where's the card? How many pints is half of a gallon?"
- The teacher turns to another student.
  - S: "Forty-six times twenty-four." T: "Student I, what did you get?"
- The teacher's utterance is unrelated to the student's utterance.
  - S: "Because I subtracted 8 from 26." T: "Okay. favorite pet. You're going to vote one time. dog, cat, fish, or bird, all right? ..."

## Rater guidance (FAQ)
1. **Simple acknowledgments** ("uh-huh", "okay") generally count as mid. The exception is when the teacher then says something completely unrelated, which is low, since it suggests the teacher is ignoring the student's contribution.
   - S: "You have to find a multiple of the two denominators." T: "Uh-huh. So remember when we just worked with fractions and not whole numbers?" → mid (acknowledges, then says something sort of related)
   - Same S. T: "Uh-huh." → mid
   - Same S. T: "Uh-huh. What was the homework for today?" → low (acknowledges, then completely unrelated)
2. **Long teacher utterances with both low and high parts:** choose the label that best describes the whole utterance. If in part of the utterance the teacher shows they are really listening to the student, choose high. Example: S: "When it says this, do you have to put the name?" T: "No. In 4th grade, these are the five – well, I want you all to know decagon, which is 10. So there's six that I want you to know and be able to name. ... What do the directions say?" → high (responds to the question in detail).
3. **Verbatim repetition** of the student's utterance is mid or high. Just repeating it without saying much else or showing consideration is mid. Adding some further consideration of the contribution (e.g. "yes", "why?") is high.
4. **Length of the teacher's utterance** should not matter much. A lengthy utterance that does not address the student's contribution is low or mid.
5. **Acceptance without follow-up:** follow-up and active listening are separate. If the teacher accepts the student's contribution, rate it high or mid depending on how much consideration they show.
6. **Student reading aloud** from the textbook is treated like any other student utterance. If the teacher engages with what was read, that is high.
7. **Very short student utterances:** code the teacher's response as much as possible irrespective of how complex the student's utterance is.
8. **The student said exactly what the teacher expected:** if the teacher builds the student's contribution into their narrative, that is generally high; if the engagement seems cursory, mid.
